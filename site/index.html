From base model to assistant

We post-trained OLMo-2 1B, a fully open base model, with AI2's published recipe: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on 866k example conversations, then direct preference optimization (DPO) on pairs of answers labeled better and worse. We saved dozens of checkpoints along the way, densest at the start, where the model changes fastest.

Learning from preferences

Preference tuning trains on pairs of answers where a judge marked one as better. It's the core of RLHF (reinforcement learning from human feedback), the method behind ChatGPT-style assistants. Whatever the judges consistently reward, the model learns, including preferences they never meant to express. Try judging a few pairs.

Training on a labeler's judgments

Three simulated labelers each follow one hidden rule. Each round, a labeler judges 16 fresh pairs of the model's own answers, and the model takes a LoRA DPO update on those judgments. Pick a labeler and train it round by round.

Under the hood
  • Each round: 16 new questions, two sampled answers each, the judge's pick, then a LoRA DPO update (β = 0.1, 3 epochs). The reference model is the model at the start of the round (iterative DPO), so changes compound.
  • Classic RLHF first trains a separate reward model on the preference pairs, then runs reinforcement learning against it. DPO learns from the same kind of pairs directly, without the separate reward model. We use DPO throughout.
  • “What the picks reveal” is a Bradley–Terry fit: a logistic regression on the differences between the two answers' traits. Traits are counted by simple rules (length in tokens, list items and headers, hedge words, confident words, exclamation marks).
  • The replays use AI2's released SFT model as a stand-in until ours is finished. They were generated on a laptop GPU (RTX 4060, about 100 seconds per round) by this script. Live training from your own picks is not built yet.

3

Reinforcement learning with a verifier

Here the training signal comes from a program instead of people. Qwen2.5-3B, a base model, is given Countdown puzzles (combine the numbers to reach a target), and a verifier scores every attempt: 1 for a correct equation, 0.1 for one that is merely well-formed. It is never shown a solution. This is RLVR, reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards, the approach behind reasoning models like DeepSeek-R1, trained here with GRPO.

Try one yourself

Use each number exactly once, with + − × ÷ and parentheses. Your answer is scored by the same verifier as the model's.

Under the hood
  • Algorithm: GRPO. Each round samples 8 attempts for each of 32 puzzles and reinforces attempts that score above their group's average. Reward: 1 for a correct equation, 0.1 for a well-formed answer. Learning rate 1e-6, no KL penalty, 900 optimizer steps, full-parameter training on one H200.
  • Puzzles come from the Countdown dataset used by TinyZero. The last 256 are held out and never trained on; all 256 are solvable (checked by brute force). Accuracy during training uses those 256 puzzles × 4 tries at temperature 1 (1,024 answers per point).
  • “How it talks” counts phrases with simple patterns. It shows how the model phrases its reasoning, not whether each check is valid.
