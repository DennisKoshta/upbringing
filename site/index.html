Upbringing

A base language model can't answer a question. It continues text. Post-training turns it into an assistant, and then into something that reasons. Here you can watch that happen to real open models, one checkpoint at a time, and then try shaping one yourself.

Act I

Birth of an assistant

We take OLMo-2 1B, a fully open base model, and run AI2's published post-training recipe on it: supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on hundreds of thousands of example conversations, then preference tuning with DPO. We saved the model dozens of times along the way. Pick a question and drag through training.

Act II

You are the reward model

DPO learns from pairs: two answers to the same question, one marked better. Whoever does the marking is the reward model, and the model will learn whatever they actually reward, which is not always what they think they reward. Be that person for a minute.

Now let DPO learn a taste

Below, three simulated labelers with simple hidden rules each judged 16 fresh pairs per round for 8 rounds. After every round, a LoRA DPO update ran on their picks: about 20 seconds per round on a laptop GPU. Watch the model drift toward each labeler's taste, and watch the fitted reward model recover the hidden rule.

Act III

The aha moment

Now there is no human at all. Qwen2.5-3B, a base model, gets arithmetic puzzles (“make 48 from 10, 55 and 17”) and a programmatic checker: 1 point for a correct equation, 0.1 for a well-formed answer, nothing else. Nothing tells it how to think. This is reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR) with GRPO: sample eight attempts per puzzle, reinforce the ones that beat their siblings.
